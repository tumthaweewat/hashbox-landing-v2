## Why Thai context is a criterion, not a nice-to-have {#thai}

Three things break foreign-first AI projects in Thailand: customers expect to talk to you on **LINE**, not email or a web widget; personal data falls under **PDPA**, which decides whether you can send it to a public LLM at all; and **Thai text** — no spaces between words, mixed Thai-English, transliterated brand names — degrades models and retrieval tuned on English. Only Botnoi states a Thai-trained model; only Data Wow and Hashbox state PDPA-driven design; only Botnoi and Hashbox state LINE delivery. Ask every firm how they handle all three before you ask about the model.

## 7 questions to ask before you sign {#questions}

1. **What is the price of the first engagement, and what does it include?** If it is not on the website, get it in writing.
2. **Will you tell me not to build?** Ask for an example where the assessment ended a project.
3. **Who owns the code, prompts, vector database and fine-tuned weights when we part ways?**
4. **Where does our data go?** Which model, which region, and what happens to PDPA-sensitive fields.
5. **Show me a Thai-language conversation your system handles badly.** Honest firms have one.
6. **Who maintains it in month 4?** Care plan price, response time, what "monitoring" means.
7. **Can I call one client?** Logos are not references.

## FAQ {#faq}

### What AI is available in Thailand?

Four buckets: ready-to-use Thai AI platforms (Botnoi chat/voicebots, Amity Solutions agents, VISAI models and APIs); consulting firms that build custom systems to production (Hashbox, DBot, Data Wow, Sertis); enterprise programmes (Accenture, Deloitte, Adastra, PwC, KPMG); and in-house AI teams at large banks and retailers, which you cannot hire. If you want a system built for your business, start with the two hireable groups and the 7 criteria above.

### Are AI consultants in high demand in Thailand?

Demand is visibly growing — LINE automation, PDPA-compliant chatbots and RAG knowledge bases have moved from experiments to line items in ordinary SME budgets, and enterprise houses keep expanding local AI practices (Deloitte acquired Thai consultancy Appsynth in 2025). The practical consequence for buyers: good boutique teams book out, so ask about start dates early — and be more sceptical, not less, of anyone instantly available who promises everything.

### How much does AI consulting cost in Thailand?
Published prices in 2026: Hashbox from THB 60,000 for an ROI assessment (PoC 200,000, production 500,000), DBot USD 20–45/hour with a USD 10k minimum. Enterprise practices (Accenture, Deloitte, Sertis, Adastra) do not publish prices; programmes typically start around THB 2M. Budget separately for LLM API usage, hosting and a monthly care plan.

### Which AI consulting company in Bangkok is right for an SME?
One that publishes prices, runs an assessment before building, ships to production and hands over source code — on this list Hashbox (7/7) and DBot (5/7) meet most of that; Botnoi is the strongest choice if the need is specifically a Thai LINE chatbot on a managed platform. Enterprise houses are built for programmes, not a first automation.

### Do the Big-4 do AI consulting in Thailand?
Yes — Accenture (3,890 staff in Thailand), Deloitte (Bangkok office, acquired Appsynth in 2025), PwC and KPMG all run AI practices, mostly strategy-to-managed-service programmes for large enterprises with existing ERP estates. None publish Thai pricing or Thai case studies on their AI pages.

### What should an AI consultant deliver besides the model?
A written ROI case before the build, a production system integrated with your channels (LINE, CRM, ERP), PDPA-compliant data handling, monitoring and cost guardrails, documentation and source code you own, and training so your team can run it. If the proposal is a strategy deck plus workshops, that is advisory, not delivery.
